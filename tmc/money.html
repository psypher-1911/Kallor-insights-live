WHY YOU SHOULD CARE

Four companies sell the same thing - a managed trip - and keep between 4 cents and 8 cents of every dollar a client spends. Where the rest goes is the whole argument: who is carrying too many people, too much technology, too much head office. Read left to right: what clients buy → what the company keeps → what it spends it on → what is left.

The gold so far

Take rate is the biggest lever, and CTM has the smallest. CTM keeps 6.8c of each TTV dollar; GBT 7.5c; FLT Corporate 9.3c. Lifting CTM to GBT's rate on A$9.8B is ~A$70M of revenue - more than half of underlying EBITDA - with no cost taken out.

Corporate travel is a 20%+ EBITDA business when run at scale. FLT Corporate 21.9%, GBT adjusted 20%. CTM's 17% underlying is the gap to close; its pre-tax 3.8% says most of the leak is below EBITDA (remediation, interest, D&A) - a one-off problem, not a structural one, if the remediation ends.

Technology is where the four disagree most. GBT spends 19% of revenue on it, FLT 9% (group, incl. shops). Whichever CTM reports decides the AI-to-CM argument: at 9% the pitch is "automate the people line"; at 19% it is "we already spend it - make it show".

People per revenue dollar is unknown for exactly the two pure TMCs. GBT buries it in cost of revenue; CTM's is in the Financial Report. That single line - people % of revenue, CTM vs GBT - is the first thing the import door should fill.

Paste the reply into /_tmc/import. It lands as provisional; a line with a page beats a line without one; a real value replaces an estimate; null never overwrites a number.
Currencies are as reported and not converted - the ratios are the comparison, not the dollars.
